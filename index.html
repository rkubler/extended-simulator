Extended Shot Peening Coverage Simulator

The nozzle sweeps the part along parallel lines, back and forth, with a step δ between lines: horizontal lines for a 0° raster, vertical lines for a 90° raster. A jet of width ΔX overlaps neighbouring lines by ΔX/δ, so the number of impacts can vary from line to line in stripes; crossing the rasters at 0° and 90° turns the stripes into a finer checkerboard. The page builds the map of impacts per point across the part, then the coverage τ = 100·(1 − e−λ), and compares it with the average model Kδ = K·ΔX/δ.

Parameters

Defaults: reference spreadsheet model (ṁ = 1.5 kg/min, ρ = 2.2 g/cm³, D = 150 µm, r = 10 µm, ΔX = 20 mm, V = 300 mm/s) with a Gaussian jet, a 15 mm step and 3 rasters at 0° alternating with 3 at 90°. Set the 90° rasters to 0 to recover parallel passes only. Changing a parameter restarts the simulation.

How the extended model works

  1. E[D³]: mean of the cube of the shot diameterE[·] is the mean (expected value) over all the shots, each shot counting once. The mass of a shot is proportional to D³, so the mean shot mass needs the mean of D³, not the cube of the mean diameter: large shots weigh much more, so E[D³] > (mean D)³. Identical shots (σ = 0): E[D³] = D³. Log-normal sizes with mass median D₅₀: E[D³] = D₅₀³·e^(−4.5σ²). From a sieve analysis (mass fractions wᵢ of classes Dᵢ): E[D³] = Σwᵢ / Σ(wᵢ/Dᵢ³). Likewise E[D²] (mean of D²) sets the mean dent area.
  2. Shots per secondṄ = ṁ / m̄ₚ, with the mean shot mass m̄ₚ = ρ·π/6·E[D³]
  3. Area of one denta = π·E[r²] = π·c²·E[D²], c = r₅₀/D₅₀
  4. K of a single line (step δ = ΔX)K = a·Ṅ / (V·ΔX): the original spreadsheet model
  5. Average K of a raster with step δKδ = a·Ṅ / (V·δ) = K·ΔX/δ (each point is crossed by ΔX/δ lines on average)
  6. Local impacts per pointλ(x,y) = a·(Ṅ/V)·[Σ0° lines g(y − yj) + Σ90° lines g(x − xj)], g = jet profile across the line (∫g = 1); uniform: 1/ΔX; Gaussian: σj = ΔX/4
  7. Local and mean coverageτ(x,y) = 1 − e^(−λ(x,y)); the mean of τ is always ≤ 1 − e^(−mean λ): stripes cost coverage
  8. Stripe amplitude, one raster(λ max − λ min) / λ mean; Gaussian jet: ≈ 4·e^(−2π²(σj/δ)²), below 3 % for δ ≤ 2σj = ΔX/2
  9. Coverage above 100 % (NF L 06-832)Base time tb: the time to dent 98 % of the surface (here from the stripe-corrected model). Full coverage, 100 %, is 1.25·tb (an option sets it to tb instead); a coverage C means C/100 times the time of full coverage (200 % = twice that time). On an even treatment, 1.25·tb gives λ₁₀₀ = 1.25·ln 50 = 4.89 impacts per point, 99.25 % dented (without the factor: λ₁₀₀ = ln 50 = 3.91, 98 % dented). With stripes the base time is longer than on an even treatment. The readout “Coverage (NF L 06-832)” is the mean dented area up to the base time tb (98 %), goes from 98 % to 100 % between tb and 1.25·tb, and is 100·t / (1.25·tb) above.
  10. Crossed rasters at 0° and 90°The 0° lines make stripes along y, the 90° lines along x, so λ(x,y) = λ₀(y) + λ₉₀(x). The mean coverage factorises: τ̄ = 1 − mean(e^(−λ₀))·mean(e^(−λ₉₀)). Without offset and for the same total number of rasters, crossing never gives less coverage than parallel passes: the weak stripes of one direction fall across the strong stripes of the other instead of on top of themselves. The average model only sees the total n = n₀ + n₉₀.
